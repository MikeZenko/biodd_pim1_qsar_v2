A Release-Pinned and Temporally Sealed PIM1 QSAR
                    Benchmark
        Temporal reliability under joint chemical, cohort, and assay-provenance shift

                                              Tair Narynov


                                                 Abstract
          QSAR performance estimated from public bioactivity data can be distorted by release drift,
     assay mixing, structure leakage, and repeated test-set use. We created a release-pinned benchmark
     of human PIM1 biochemical inhibition from ChEMBL 37. Standardization and strict assay/target
     filters produced 1,125 measurement events for 1,087 compounds. An outcome-blind count rule
     fixed 2018 as the cutoff, yielding 803 development compounds and a sealed test of 284 compounds
     first meeting the strict cohort definition in 2019–2025. Under a prespecified, modest tuning
     budget, seven fixed-representation/baseline candidates and four graph-neural-network variants
     shared repeated scaffold-disjoint assignments; all selection was confined to development data.
     The selected Ridge (ECFP6 + RDKit2D) achieved repeated-CV RMSE 0.742 pIC50 and one-time
     temporal RMSE 0.972 (95% scaffold-cluster bootstrap CI 0.872–1.075), with pooled R2 = 0.470.
     This aggregate concealed time-local degradation: RMSE increased from 0.795 in 2019 to 1.261
     in 2024, by 0.466 pIC50 (95% scaffold-cluster CI 0.190– 0.733). However, a document-source
     sensitivity interval for pooled R2 widened to -0.007– 0.682, and after centering within documents
     with at least three compounds, R2 was -0.031 and Spearman ρ was 0.303. A training-defined
     Tanimoto domain described structural shift but did not identify error-prone predictions (ρ = 0.042
     between absolute error and neighbor similarity). Development and temporal labels shared no
     assay or document identifiers, so the observed decline represents inseparable chemical, cohort,
     and assay-provenance shift. A development-derived residual reference band was 2.504 pIC50 wide
     (approximately a 319-fold IC50 span) and covered 81.3% of temporal cases when defined at a
     90% development target, limiting its potency-threshold decision value. The best graph model
     obtained development RMSE 0.893; under the fixed budget, the evaluated graph variants did not
     justify their added flexibility, but this does not rank fixed and learned representations generally.
     The principal finding is that sealed temporal evaluation revealed reliability failures obscured
     by pooled performance. This retrospective biochemical-potency benchmark does not establish
     prospective activity, selectivity, cellular efficacy, ADMET properties, or therapeutic value.

Keywords: PIM1; QSAR; ChEMBL; temporal validation; scaffold split; graph neural network;
assay provenance; applicability domain


1    Introduction
The proviral integration site for Moloney murine leukemia virus (PIM) family comprises constitutively
active serine/threonine kinases PIM1, PIM2, and PIM3. PIM signaling has been implicated in
hematological and solid malignancies, making the family a continuing target of inhibitor discovery
(Nawijn et al., 2011; Tursynbay et al., 2016). A ligand-based model of PIM1 biochemical potency
can support hypothesis generation, but biochemical IC50 alone does not establish isoform selectivity,
target engagement in cells, exposure, safety, or clinical efficacy.


                                                      1
   PIM1 has an atypical ATP-binding hinge: Pro123 lacks the backbone amide donor that supplies a
canonical second hydrogen bond to the adenine moiety in many kinases (Kumar et al., 2005). More
generally, under the assumptions of simple competitive inhibition, the Cheng–Prusoff relationship
makes apparent IC50 conditional on substrate concentration (Cheng and Prusoff, 1973). Consequently,
strict target and endpoint filters can reduce ambiguity in public PIM1 data without making
measurements obtained under different ATP conditions experimentally equivalent.
   Machine-learning QSAR methods can extract useful structure–activity patterns from public
databases (Cherkasov et al., 2014). PIM1 studies have used small congeneric series for 3D-QSAR
and ligand-based virtual screening (Aouidate et al., 2018; Asati et al., 2017), and more recent work
has combined public bioactivity data with classification, docking, or generative design (Almukadi
et al., 2023; Schneider et al., 2020). Yet a model comparison is only as credible as the experiment
surrounding it. Database release changes can alter the available records; related structures can
leak across random splits; model selection can consume the purported test set; and assay or
target-confidence heterogeneity can dominate algorithmic differences.
   Graph neural networks (GNNs) offer an appealing alternative to fixed molecular fingerprints
because they learn task-specific graph representations. Directed message-passing neural networks
(D-MPNNs) have reported strong molecular-property performance (Yang et al., 2019), while graph-
isomorphism-derived operators provide expressive neighborhood aggregation (Xu et al., 2019; Hu
et al., 2020). However, learned graph representations do not universally outperform carefully tuned
fixed representations, particularly for modest, noisy bioactivity datasets (Deng et al., 2023). A
fair PIM1 comparison therefore needs shared compound identities, shared splits, development-only
tuning, and representation ablations.
   Here we construct such a benchmark. Our primary contribution is an auditable test of temporal
reliability: (i) a ChEMBL 37 snapshot with source files, release metadata, lineage, and SHA-256
checksums; (ii) a stringent PIM1 biochemical cohort; (iii) an outcome-blind publication-year cutoff
and immutable repeated scaffold assignments; and (iv) a one-time temporal evaluation, followed by
an explicitly labeled post hoc audit of performance within annual chemistry cohorts. Calibration,
residual-reference-band, scaffold-novelty, and similarity-domain diagnostics test whether common
reliability summaries remain informative under that shift. A bounded comparison of classical
ECFP6/descriptor methods, an edge-aware GINE, and a Chemprop D-MPNN is secondary. The
goal is not a new learning architecture or a general ranking of representation families; it is a
defensible account of what a target-specific model can and cannot support when chemistry and
assay provenance change over time.


2     Results
2.1   Release-pinned curation reduced target ambiguity but retained multi-assay
      heterogeneity
The archived ChEMBL 37 query returned 4,861 activities, 986 target assays, and 3,112 parent
molecules (Fig. 1). Broad record-level eligibility retained 4,380 records. The confirmatory cohort
additionally required a biochemical assay, BAO single-protein format, direct target relationship,
confidence score 9, and no assayed variant. It retained 1,125 measurement events and 1,087
standardized parent compounds (Table 1). The largest reduction arose from excluding 2,973
activity records linked to homologous rather than direct single-protein targets. This restriction is
consequential: ChEMBL defines confidence score 9 as direct assignment to a single protein and
score 8 as an assignment to a homologous single protein (Zdrazil et al., 2024).

    An explicitly post hoc audit of the pinned assay metadata found that the strict events came
from 122 assays in 102 documents. The median assay contributed only 3 events (range 1–65),
and the largest representative assay accounted for 5.8% of events. Although descriptions for
34 assays mentioned ATP, only 8 assays—covering 117/1,125 events (10.4%)—stated a numeric
ATP concentration. The reported values spanned 20–5000 µM, a 250-fold range (Supplementary
Table 7). Moreover, 97.8% of compounds had only one eligible event. Median aggregation therefore
summarized the few repeats that existed but could not harmonize most single measurements.
This limitation is substantive: direct comparisons across ChEMBL IC50 sources can contain large
inter-assay discrepancies, while careful metadata curation or matched-pair contrasts reduce but do
not eliminate them (Landrum and Riniker, 2024; Nelen et al., 2025).
    The exhaustive bridge search within the machine-readable numeric-ATP subset found only one
compound measured in the same document at two explicit ATP concentrations: 9 nM at 50 µM
ATP versus 100 nM at 5000 µM ATP, a 1.046-pIC50 decrease (Dakin et al., 2012). This n = 1
observation illustrates why ATP context may matter; it is not an assay-effect estimate or a basis for
post hoc correction.
    Structure standardization failed for 0 queried parents. Only 24 strict compounds had multiple
independent measurement events; 10 had a pIC50 range above 0.5 and 6 above 1.0. Compound labels
were medians of eligible measurement-event pIC50 values, preserving event lineage and dispersion
fields rather than silently pooling raw rows.

2.2    The count-only rule produced a demanding temporal test
The prespecified cutoff rule selected the latest year satisfying at least 750 development compounds,
250 temporal compounds, and 100 new temporal scaffolds; 2018 was the unique latest eligible
cutoff. The immutable split contains 803 development compounds from 369 scaffolds and 284
first-seen-after-cutoff compounds from 160 scaffolds. 149 temporal scaffolds were absent from
development, accounting for 251 compounds (88.4%). Thus, the temporal test is predominantly a
joint time-and-scaffold extrapolation, while retaining a small seen-scaffold subset for a prespecified
diagnostic.
    Here “first seen” refers specifically to an eligible event in the strict cohort. A post hoc cross-check
against the broader curated cohort found 4 temporal identities with an earlier, non-strict record;
those earlier records failed the direct single-protein criteria. Removing those identities left 280
temporal compounds and produced RMSE 0.976 and R2 = 0.463, so this wording correction did not
alter the substantive result.

2.3      A bounded model search did not reward added graph flexibility
All candidates used the same 3 repeated ×5-fold outer assignments. Each outer validation compound
appeared exactly once per repeat, and no Bemis–Murcko scaffold crossed an outer train/validation
boundary. Figure 2 and Table 2 report pooled out-of-fold metrics calculated separately for each
repeat and then summarized across repeats.
   The Ridge (ECFP6 + RDKit2D) ranked first with RMSE 0.742 and was therefore locked before
the temporal table was read. This development comparison used deliberately modest, prespecified
search spaces: the graph arm comprised one edge-aware GINE and one Chemprop D-MPNN, each
with and without descriptors, and did not evaluate pretraining or a broad architecture search. The
strongest evaluated graph candidate was Edge-aware GINE (graph + RDKit2D) (RMSE 0.893). Its
paired candidate-minus-selected RMSE difference was 0.070 pIC50 (95% scaffold-cluster bootstrap
CI 0.029 to 0.114). The runner-up RBF-SVR (ECFP6 + RDKit2D) was nearly tied (RMSE 0.744);
its paired RMSE difference was 0.002 (95% CI -0.021 to 0.024). Thus, selection of Ridge follows the
locked point-estimate rule and is not evidence that it is meaningfully superior to the runner-up.
Descriptor concatenation improved GINE but not the D-MPNN.

2.4   Pooled temporal performance masked cohort-level degradation
After development-only selection, the selected procedure was tuned on three scaffold-disjoint folds
within the complete development set, refit without temporal labels, and evaluated once. It achieved
RMSE 0.972 (95% CI 0.872–1.075), MAE 0.750 (95% CI 0.666–0.840), and R2 = 0.470 (95%
CI 0.311–0.582; Table 3). Relative to the repeated-CV RMSE, the temporal error changed by
0.231 pIC50 units. Confidence intervals resampled Bemis–Murcko scaffold clusters, not individual
compounds.
   The post hoc provenance audit showed an additional boundary: development labels came from
68 assays in 62 documents, whereas temporal labels came from 48 assays in 36 documents. The two
partitions shared 0 assay identifiers and 0 document identifiers. Temporal performance therefore
reflects joint chemical, calendar-cohort, and assay/source-provenance shift; these components cannot
be causally separated by this split.

gave wider sensitivity intervals: RMSE 0.787–1.150 and R2 -0.007–0.682 (Supplementary Table 8).

compounds, document-centered R2 was -0.031 with ρ = 0.303 (261 compounds in 19 documents),
and assay-centered R2 was -0.146 with ρ = 0.240 (252 compounds in 23 assays). Thus, weak
within-source ordering was not solely an artifact of annual centering.

2.5     The evaluated reliability diagnostics did not identify temporal errors
The observed-on-predicted calibration slope was 0.930 with intercept 0.382. An absolute-residual
reference band defined by the finite-sample 90% quantile of locked development OOF errors had
width 2.504 pIC50 and covered 81.3% of temporal compounds (95% scaffold-cluster CI 75.2–86.7%).
This was not a conformal predictor: its scores came from fold-specific OOF fits after development-
based candidate selection, whereas its centers came from a separate final refit. Moreover, ordinary
exchangeability between development and future cases is not justified under the observed temporal
chemical and assay/process shift (Tibshirani et al., 2019; Barber et al., 2023). The reported
coverage is therefore empirical, not a distribution-free guarantee. Even apart from formal validity,
the approximately 319-fold IC50 span has limited value for typical potency-threshold decisions.
Conformal regression remains a useful QSAR framework when its construction and assumptions
match the intended deployment setting (Svensson et al., 2018).
    The applicability-domain threshold was the fifth percentile of development compounds’ mean
Tanimoto similarity to their five nearest non-self development neighbors. 154 temporal compounds
fell below it. Absolute error and mean neighbor similarity had Spearman ρ = 0.042. Inside- and
outside-domain RMSE and MAE were nearly indistinguishable (RMSE 0.954 versus 0.988; Table 3),

The absolute potency difference between each temporal compound and its most similar development
neighbor correlated with model absolute error (ρ = 0.271; 95% scaffold-cluster bootstrap CI 0.122–
0.411). This nearest-neighbor potency gap uses the temporal outcome and is therefore not an
available prediction-time uncertainty score. It instead identifies response-space discordance—the
continuous analogue of an activity-cliff problem—as one contributor to failure of the purely structural
domain (van Tilborg et al., 2022).

3    Discussion
This study changes the evidential basis of the PIM1 analysis by replacing a mutable or ambiguously
dated dataset with a release-pinned, lineage-preserving cohort and by enforcing immutable time and
scaffold boundaries before model comparison. Candidate selection was completed without temporal
outcomes, after which the locked procedure used the temporal set exactly once. The code paths, null
temporal-input hashes in development manifests, start record, and completed evaluation manifest
make that operational boundary auditable; they are a software control, not a claim that the label
file was cryptographically inaccessible.
    The most decision-relevant result is not the pooled temporal R2 , but the deterioration within
successive chemistry cohorts. Error rose materially from 2019 to 2024, and the post hoc year-aware
analysis found weak and unstable within-cohort ordering after 2019. Differences among annual
mean potencies restored variation when cohorts were pooled and therefore made the aggregate R2
more favorable than recent-cohort prioritization alone. This does not make the pooled estimate
invalid; it shows that a single pooled statistic answers a different question from whether a model
can rank compounds within the chemistry available in a particular year. Both views are needed for
discovery decisions. The added dependence audit sharpens this conclusion: source-level clustering
widened uncertainty, while error still increased in a linear time summary and within-document and
within-assay ordering remained weak. The evidence supports degradation under joint shift, but not
a source-independent causal time effect.
    The negative reliability results reinforce that distinction. The locked Tanimoto threshold detected
structural distance—more than half of temporal compounds lay outside it—but did not identify high-
error predictions. Likewise, the development-residual reference band was wide, undercovered after
temporal shift, and had limited threshold-decision value. Similarity and cross-validated residuals
therefore described aspects of the shift without yielding a useful prediction- level reliability screen.
This failure is informative: standard diagnostics that look sensible in development may not remain
operational under combined chemical, assay, and cohort shift. The positive association between
nearest-neighbor potency discordance and model error further indicates that response discontinuities
and assay-dependent labels, not distance alone, contribute to the failures.
    More elaborate structural-domain methods can sometimes separate uncertainty reliability on
nonrandom test sets (Hosni et al., 2026). The negative result here therefore concerns this simple
mean-5-neighbor threshold, not the entire applicability-domain concept; it motivates direct validation
of each proposed reliability rule under the deployment shift it is meant to address.
    The representation comparison is secondary. Under the prespecified modest search budget, none
of the four evaluated graph variants improved on the leading ECFP6-plus-descriptor candidates.
This pattern is compatible with the lower data efficiency reported for some task-trained molecular
representations (Deng et al., 2023), but the present experiment did not vary training-set size and
therefore cannot identify data scarcity as the cause. The graph inputs contained chemically informed
atom and bond attributes; unlike ECFP6, however, they did not encode radius-3 substructures a
priori. Pretraining could alter the comparison, although published benefits are setting-dependent
and downstream hyperparameters can be at least as consequential on small tasks (Sun et al.,
2022). These results describe the four evaluated variants under the locked budget, not a sample-size

threshold or a general representation ranking.
   Publication-year validation is more decision-relevant than a random split and more realistic than
scaffold CV alone, but it remains retrospective. Because public ChEMBL publication dates can
pool distinct groups and projects, this split enforces a chronological information boundary without
reproducing the feedback-driven order in which compounds are designed and tested within one
medicinal-chemistry program (Sheridan, 2013; Landrum et al., 2023). It therefore combines chemical
novelty with changes in assay, reporting, and deposition practice rather than isolating prospective
model decay. The high prevalence of new scaffolds and complete assay/document turnover make
those joint shifts explicit.
   Several limitations bound interpretation. ChEMBL harmonizes reported endpoints but does
not make assays experimentally identical. Even confidence-9 biochemical IC50 values can differ in
substrate, ATP concentration, construct, protocol, and laboratory (Landrum and Riniker, 2024;
Nelen et al., 2025). Numeric ATP concentration was recoverable for only 10.4% of strict events, only
18 compounds bridged assays, and 82.2% of events belonged to scaffolds seen in a single assay. ATP
normalization would additionally require the relevant kinetic parameters and inhibition mechanism;
assay-effect estimation is confounded by chemistry and time. We therefore did not attempt a post
hoc Cheng–Prusoff correction, assay random effect, or causal decomposition.
   Document year is a publication-linked proxy for availability, not the date a result entered ChEMBL
or became accessible to a model developer. The test is chronologically held out but arises from the
same public database and literature-curation ecosystem; it is not independent external-laboratory or
prospective validation. Median aggregation reduces replicate influence but can hide multimodality,
so event-level lineage remains essential. Hyperparameter grids were deliberately bounded, the GNN
arm did not estimate optimization-seed variability, and it covers neither every architecture nor
pretraining strategy. Applicability-domain and calibration analyses diagnose this dataset; they do
not prove uncertainty validity after distribution shift. Finally, the endpoint is PIM1 biochemical
potency only. No inference is warranted about PIM2/PIM3 selectivity, kinome off-targets, cell
permeability, toxicity, pharmacokinetics, or efficacy.
   The decisive next study is a prospectively registered evaluation in which predictions are frozen
before new outcomes are generated. It should include deliberately selected new scaffolds, a single
biochemical assay system with reported ATP conditions, matched measurements against PIM1,
PIM2, and PIM3, and a cellular substrate-phosphorylation or target-engagement assay. Kinome
selectivity and ADME should remain separately defined endpoints. Such a study would test whether
the present error and reliability estimates translate to genuine discovery use.


4     Methods
4.1   Release snapshot and source provenance
Data were queried from the ChEMBL REST interface for target CHEMBL2147 by requiring the
standardized IC50 endpoint, exact equality, nM units, standard flag 1, and a nonmissing positive
value. The snapshot records ChEMBL 37, release date 1 May 2026, the REST base URL, the official
release-status response, and the official FTP release README, notes, and checksums. Activities,
referenced assay records, target metadata, and parent-molecule records were written as compressed
JSON lines or JSON with per-file SHA-256 values. A curation manifest links every downstream
table to those hashes. ChEMBL is described by Zdrazil et al. (2024).




                                                 10
4.2   Structure and activity curation
Parent structures were processed with chembl-structure-pipeline==1.2.4, which implements
ChEMBL-like structure standardization on RDKit (Bento et al., 2020). The standardized parent
was serialized as canonical isomeric SMILES and identified by InChIKey. Bemis–Murcko scaffolds
were generated with RDKit (Bemis and Murcko, 1996); acyclic compounds were assigned a common
explicit acyclic group. The structure pipeline version was checked at runtime and no fallback
standardizer was permitted.
   Broad eligibility required human PIM1 target metadata, exact positive IC50 in nM, standard
flag 1, no potential-duplicate flag, no data-validity warning, a valid document year, concordant
assay target, and a standardizable parent structure. The strict primary cohort additionally required
assay type B, BAO format BAO_0000357 (single protein), target relationship D, confidence score 9,
and no variant accession or mutation. Equivalent source events were identified from parent, target,
record, document, normalized assay description, endpoint, format, assay type, relation, value, and
units; their activity identifiers remained in a lineage table.
   Activity was transformed as

                                    pIC50 = 9 − log10 (IC50 [nM]).

Compound labels were the median eligible event pIC50 . Counts, standard deviation, median absolute
deviation, range, first and last document year, event IDs, assay IDs, and document IDs were retained.
The prespecified high-tail sensitivity cohort excluded events with pIC50 > 10.

4.3   Post hoc assay-metadata audit
After the sealed evaluation, we audited assay context directly from the pinned strict event table;
this audit was descriptive and did not alter labels, model selection, or predictions. Events were
grouped by representative assay, document, standardized-parent identity, and Bemis–Murcko scaffold.
We counted case-insensitive whole-word mentions of ATP. Explicit positive concentrations were
extracted only when a numeric nM, µM, or mM value immediately modified ATP in the archived
description; values were normalized to µM. Descriptions stating ATP at Km without a numeric
value were tabulated separately. Missing text was never interpreted as a constant or absent ATP
condition.
   Assay-size distributions, cross-assay compound bridges, and the proportion of events whose scaffold
occurred in only one assay were computed without reference to prediction error. Development
provenance used label events at or before the 2018 cutoff; quarantined later records for development
identities were excluded. All events for first-seen-after-cutoff identities defined temporal provenance.
Assay and document intersections were then enumerated across partitions. Finally, we exhaustively
searched for identities measured within the same document in different assays with different explicit
ATP concentrations; no pair was selected by the direction or magnitude of its potency change.
Because assay conditions, chemistry, and time were sparsely bridged, this audit was not used to fit
an assay-effect model or normalize IC50 .

4.4   Outcome-blind temporal sealing
Compound identity was assigned to development or temporal test by its first eligible strict
measurement-event document year. Before any candidate model was fit, a count-only feasibil-
ity table enumerated each potential cutoff. The locked rule chose the latest cutoff with at least 750


                                                  11
development compounds, 250 temporal compounds, and 100 scaffolds present only in the temporal
partition. No activity value was permitted in cutoff selection. The selected cutoff was 2018, so
development used events through 2018 and the test contained compounds first meeting the strict
cohort definition from 2019 onward. Post-2018 records for identities already assigned to development
were quarantined and never updated training labels. Development, temporal, cross-cutoff, and
fold-assignment files were made immutable through a split manifest containing source and output
hashes.
   This construction is a retrospective publication-year temporal split, not a prospective experiment.
It follows the rationale that time-split validation can better approximate future prediction than
random resampling (Sheridan, 2013), while recognizing that simulated or historical splits have their
own biases (Landrum et al., 2023).

4.5   Fixed molecular representations and classical candidates
ECFP6 fingerprints were generated with radius 3 and 2,048 bits (Rogers and Hahn, 2010). A
23-variable RDKit descriptor vector contained molecular weight, calculated logP, hydrogen-bond
counts, rotatable bonds, ring and aromatic-ring counts, heteroatom and heavy-atom counts, fraction
sp3 , TPSA, Labute ASA, Balaban J, Bertz complexity, molecular connectivity indices, QED, formal
charge, and related atom-count descriptors. The exact ordered descriptor list is stored in the feature
manifest.
   Seven prespecified fixed-representation or baseline candidates were evaluated: a training-mean
baseline, unweighted Tanimoto k-nearest-neighbor regression on ECFP6, Ridge regression, random
forest on ECFP6, random forest on ECFP6 plus descriptors, gradient boosting on the combined rep-
resentation, and RBF support-vector regression on the combined representation. Median imputation
and, where appropriate, descriptor scaling were inside each scikit-learn pipeline. Hyperparameter
grids are reported in Supplementary Table 5.

4.6   Graph neural networks
Four graph candidates crossed two backends with two feature variants: molecular graph alone
and graph plus the same 23 RDKit descriptors. The custom edge-aware GINE used explicit atom
and bond features, three message-passing layers, hidden dimension 128, residual updates, batch
normalization, mean pooling, a two-layer feed-forward head, and dropout 0.1. Its edge-aware
convolution followed the GINE operator used in graph representation learning (Hu et al., 2020); the
implementation used PyTorch Geometric (Fey and Lenssen, 2019).
   The second backend used Chemprop 2.2.4 bond-directed message passing, mean aggregation,
hidden dimension 128, depth 3, and a matched 128-dimensional feed-forward head (Yang et al.,
2019; Graff et al., 2026). Both backends used batch size 64, Adam-family optimization, target
scaling estimated from the current training partition, maximum 80 epochs, and patience 10 with
a minimum RMSE improvement of 0.001. Descriptor variants median-imputed and standardized
descriptors from the current training rows only.

4.7   Nested repeated scaffold validation and model selection
Development validation used three repeats of five-fold StratifiedGroupKFold. Bemis–Murcko
scaffold was the group and ranked pIC50 values were divided into five approximately equal strata.
Seeds 1101, 1102, and 1103 created one immutable assignment table shared across all model families.
Within each outer training partition, classical grids used three scaffold-disjoint inner folds with

                                                 12
all preprocessing refit inside the pipeline. This fully nested design prevents the selection bias that
results when tuning and assessment reuse the same observations (Varma and Simon, 2006).
   For GNNs, the same outer partitions were used. Each outer training set was divided into three
inner scaffold folds. Early stopping was performed independently on each inner validation fold; the
median of the three selected epoch counts was then used for a fresh fit on the entire outer training
partition. Outer validation labels were never monitored. Fixed GNN architectural settings bounded
the comparison and kept every outer fold strictly assessment-only.
   After completion, selected epoch counts and assigned refit durations were read from all graph
checkpoint manifests, and each companion prediction hash was reverified. We compared every inner
selected epoch and outer refit duration with the fixed 80-epoch ceiling. This checkpoint audit did
not reconstruct how many epochs each inner loop evaluated before early stopping; it was descriptive
and did not trigger new training or candidate changes.
   RMSE was the prespecified primary metric; MAE and R2 were secondary. For each repeat,
metrics were computed after pooling its complete set of 803 out-of-fold predictions. Candidates were
ranked by mean repeat-level RMSE, with model name as an exact-tie breaker. For descriptive paired
comparisons, predictions were first averaged across repeats for each compound, then scaffold clusters
were resampled 10,000 times to estimate candidate-minus-selected RMSE and MAE intervals. The
sealed temporal table was not read during selection.

4.8   Final fitting, temporal metrics, uncertainty, and domain
The selected classical candidate underwent a final three-fold, scaffold-disjoint grid search on all
development compounds and was refit before temporal prediction. Had a GNN been selected, the
prespecified procedure would have averaged three development-only refits with seeds 2101–2103;
that conditional procedure was implemented before the temporal evaluation. Primary temporal
RMSE, MAE, and R2 were accompanied by 95% percentile intervals from 10,000 bootstrap samples
of temporal Bemis–Murcko scaffolds (seed 3101).
   After the sealed primary evaluation, we performed a post hoc descriptive audit by first eligible
record year; it was not used to select, tune, or refit the model. Annual RMSE, R2 , and Spearman
correlation were calculated separately. The fraction of observed temporal variance between years
was the weighted between-cohort sum of squares divided by the total observed sum of squares. To
separate within-cohort ordering from annual mean differences, observed and predicted pIC50 values
were each centered by their corresponding first-record-year mean before pooled R2 and Spearman
correlation were calculated. We report this centered analysis for all temporal years and, to describe
a horizon at least two years beyond the 2018 cutoff, for 2020–2025. Small annual cohorts, especially
2025, make individual-year estimates unstable.
   A separate, hash-linked post hoc robustness audit consumed the immutable temporal predictions
without fitting, tuning, or changing any prediction. It (i) checked strict-cohort first-record years
against the broader curated event history; (ii) repeated primary metric intervals by resampling
sorted assay-source and document-source sets as dependence sensitivities; (iii) bootstrapped the
2019-to-2024 RMSE difference and the individual-level linear slope of absolute error on first-record
year; and (iv) centered observed and predicted pIC50 within assay or document groups with at
least three compounds. Source-set labels preserved the two multi-document and four multi-assay
compound labels rather than assigning them arbitrarily to one source. All resampling used 10,000
replicates; these post hoc intervals characterize dependence sensitivity and do not replace the
prespecified scaffold-cluster interval.
   We applied the finite-sample order-statistic correction used in absolute-residual conformal intervals


                                                  13
to the selected model’s locked repeat-0 development OOF residuals. The resulting symmetric band
is reported only as a reproducible development-residual reference: its scores came from fold-specific
OOF models after development-based candidate selection, while its centers came from a separate
final refit. Moreover, the exchangeability condition required by ordinary conformal validity is not
justified for the chronologically shifted cases (Barber et al., 2023). Weighted conformal methods can
address pure covariate shift under additional assumptions, including a stable conditional outcome
distribution and a known or estimable train/test density ratio (Tibshirani et al., 2019); those
assumptions were neither established nor used here. Temporal coverage was therefore evaluated
empirically and scaffold-cluster bootstrapped. Calibration was summarized by the slope and intercept
from observed-on-predicted linear regression and by five equal-count prediction bins.
    The applicability domain was defined entirely from training structures. For every development
compound, mean Tanimoto similarity to its five nearest non-self development neighbors was
calculated; the fifth percentile was locked as the threshold. Each temporal compound’s mean
and maximum similarity to development, nearest neighbor, and in/out-domain status were recorded.
We additionally report error–similarity correlations and metrics by in/out-domain and new/seen-
scaffold groups. Small subgroup comparisons are descriptive rather than hypothesis tests. For
explanation only, we also calculated each temporal compound’s absolute pIC50 difference from its
most similar development neighbor and scaffold-cluster bootstrapped its Spearman association with
model absolute error.

4.9   Software, integrity checks, and reproducibility
Classical analysis used Python 3.12.3, RDKit 2026.3.3, NumPy 2.5.1, pandas 3.0.3, and scikit-
learn 1.9.0. GNN analysis used PyTorch 2.12.1+cpu, PyTorch Geometric 2.8.0, and Chemprop 2.2.4
in an isolated CPU environment. Environment manifests record executable paths, installed-package
inventories, platform details, and hashes. Unit and contract tests verify curation filters, standard-
ization behavior, split disjointness, assignment inventories, training-only transforms, resumable
checkpoints, model-selection blindness, and one-time temporal artifact sealing. Every production
stage writes atomic files and a completion marker. SHA-256-linked manifests cover the release,
curation, split, selection, temporal-evaluation, post hoc robustness-audit, and manuscript stages;
the GNN benchmark records its fixed contract and fold/checkpoint inventory.


5     Data and code availability
The complete reproducibility archive is prepared for submission as supplementary material. It
contains the exact source snapshot, release metadata, curation tables, event lineage, immutable
split, candidate predictions, selection record, temporal predictions, figures, environment mani-
fests, and manuscript source. The hash-linked post hoc audit additionally contains source-set
assignments, strict-versus-broad chronology flags, and all alternative cluster intervals. The file
ARTIFACT_INDEX.md identifies confirmatory v2 artifacts and clearly separates them from legacy
exploratory results. A versioned public mirror and archival DOI will be released with the accepted
manuscript.


6     Conclusion
Release pinning and sealing converted this from an algorithm demonstration into an auditable
reliability test. Pooled temporal performance remained moderate, but source-cluster sensitivity

                                                 14
widened its uncertainty; error increased over time and within-year, within-document, and within-
assay ranking was weak amid inseparable chemical and assay-provenance turnover. The similarity
domain did not identify high-error predictions; the residual reference band was wide and undercovered.
Under the prespecified budget, graph models did not justify added flexibility, a conclusion limited
to those configurations. The decisive next test is prospective, assay-consistent chemistry.
